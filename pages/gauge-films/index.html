Upright G1

What it does. Dropped from 300 mm in a random orientation with a random spin, wheels unpowered. A design with a tail then gets 10 s of scripted righting moves. The reading is the share of 200 falls that end on the wheels, at three floor grips, the worst counting. Left: a fall that ends upright. Right: one that stays down.

Look for. Do the bounces and tumbles look like a real 300 mm drop? Could a real tail servo push this hard, this fast (6 rad/s, the tail torque knob)? A fall counts only by its final pose, so a slow, ugly righting scores the same as a clean one.

Step G2

What it does. Drives at a step 0.5 m/s, square on within 10 degrees, for 4 s. A pass is on top and on its wheels. A tail pushes down when the robot stalls nose up, or kicks when it stalls level. 20 tries a rung, nose first and tail first, the better counting. The reading is the height where the pass rate, smoothed so it never rises with height, falls to 90 %. Left: the tallest rung below the reading. Right: the next rung up.

Look for. Is it climbing, or is the tail throwing it up the step? A real servo stalls under load. Does the failure look like the real one (nose caught on the lip, or flipping over backwards)? The step edge is rounded and the floor never gives.

Turn G3

What it does. Spins in place: left wheels back, right wheels forward, both at 60 % of no-load speed, until it has turned 180 degrees (6 s at most). The reading is the diameter of the circle round the starting point that holds everything the body swept. Smaller is better. Dashed ring: the final reading. Solid ring: the sweep so far. Left: from above. Right: the same run, three-quarter view.

Look for. A spin that drifts reads bigger than its footprint, since the circle stays round the start. The sim's skid-steer slip (chi about 1.5 to 1.8) is close to published tank and rover measurements (about 1.5), but the wheels here are rigid.

Hill up G4

What it does. Drives 2.5 m up a slope at 0.5 m/s. The floor stays flat and gravity tilts, so these frames are turned to match. Grip is x2.5 so the robot tips before it slides: this gauge reads the tip limit, not traction. A pass gets there under 30 degrees off the slope. 5 degree rungs; a tail may hold a braced pose. Left: the reading. Right: 5 degrees steeper.

Look for. Real floors and tyres slide well before x2.5 grip, so a real hill limit will be lower. Does the failure look like going over backwards (the real danger), or a stall?

Hill down G5

What it does. As the hill up, driving down. It also fails if it slides: the body running ahead of its wheels for 0.2 s, or faster than twice the set speed. Left: the reading. Right: 5 degrees steeper.

Look for. Going over nose first is the failure this guards against. Is the braced tail actually doing anything?

Crest G6

What it does. Crawls at 0.15 m/s over a ridge: two planks meeting at a rounded peak 150 mm high, each at the rung's angle. A pass is over and on its wheels. Fails: stuck, stuck with its belly on the ridge, or tipped. 5 degree rungs. Left: the reading. Right: 5 degrees steeper.

Look for. Belly clearance and wheelbase decide this one. Is the failure a hang-up on the peak, or a tip on the way down?

Push G7 (realistic run)

What it does. Pushes a 200 x 260 x 150 mm box of the rung's mass 0.5 m at 0.3 m/s within 6 s, holding its heading. Fails: stalled, tipped, or rode up (its front 40 mm up onto the box). Mass rungs 0.5 to 12 kg, the worst grip counting. Left: the reading. Right: the next rung.

Look for. This is the gauge that fought the step: a curved, climbing nose rides up the box. Zach's call (2026-11-02) replaces it with the tow.

Tow G7 (new)

What it does. Drags a 300 x 300 x 60 mm sled of the rung's mass 0.5 m at 0.3 m/s within 8 s, on a 250 mm rope from a hitch on the body's rear at axle height. Fails: stalled, or tipped past 45 degrees (a wheelie onto its back). Not in the realistic run: its reading here is quick, 6 tries a rung at x1 grip. Left: the reading. Right: the next rung.

Look for. Does the pull lift the nose? A real hitch, rope and sled on carpet or concrete will differ: the sled's friction scales with the floor's here. Today's sweep reads it properly.
